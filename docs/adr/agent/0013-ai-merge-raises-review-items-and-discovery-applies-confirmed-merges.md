# AI merge asks about near duplicates, raises Review items, and Discovery applies a confirmed merge

AI merge lives in Discovery (`createAiMerge`), which depends on the AI runner and Review packages (neither imports Discovery). It runs in the Pipeline after Matching and before scoring, over every mergeable Job of the user (New or Shortlisted), not only the Jobs of the run, so a pair whose call failed is asked again by the next run of any source.

Candidate pairs, both read from Discovery's own tables: near matches (same normalized company and city, title word Jaccard at or above `AI_MERGE_JACCARD`, default 0.6; `nearBucket` and `titleOverlap` in `keys.ts`) and `merge_conflicts` rows (the Jobs the keys hit, resolved to the Job they were folded into). A pair is skipped when both Jobs already have an application status (the injected `hasApplication`, wired by the Tracker), when a never-merge pair covers it (a Posting of one Job is barred from the other, seen through Jobs folded into either), when an ai_merge Review item exists for it in any status, or when an earlier answer said it is not the same job. The last one is a deviation from the ticket text (which stored nothing for a "not the same" answer): without it every run would ask, and pay for, the same pairs again. It is the table `discovery.merge_checks` (unique per user and Job pair, smaller id first); the call log still holds the call.

The call goes through `Ai.run` with the feature name `ai_merge` (logged, cost kept, runner-wide failures end the pass after three in a row, a bad answer for one pair does not). It has its own cap, `AI_MERGE_MAX_CALLS_PER_RUN` (default 20), separate from `AI_MAX_CALLS_PER_RUN`: a throughput limit, not a spend cap, the pairs left over wait for the next run. The run's `aiCalls` and `aiCostUsd` add both kinds together; `aiMergeItems` counts the items the run created. One Postgres advisory lock per pair (taken with try-lock, held through the AI call, released with the transaction) keeps two workers from asking the same pair; the item and the check marker are written in that transaction, and the unique index on the item is the last guard. The item subject is `{ jobA, jobB }` with the smaller id first, so one pair is one item in either order. The item records the model the runner reported.

Nothing merges until the user confirms. Review's `decide` calls the applier the API maps to the kind (`aiMerge.apply`), in the decision's transaction. Disagree writes a never-merge pair for every Posting of each Job against the other Job, so the pair is never asked again. Confirm locks both Job rows in id order and folds the newer Job into the older one: its Postings move to the older Job and its row stays with `merged_into` set, keeping its company and URL keys. A later Posting that hits a folded Job's key is attached to the Job it was folded into (Discovery resolves `merged_into`), which closes the URL key chain from the #9 review without a key table: the older Job may hold only one URL key, the folded one keeps the other as an alias. Ingest takes a share lock on the Jobs its keys hit, so it waits for a merge that is moving them and then sees the result; a Posting is never left on a folded Job. Folded Jobs are hidden from `listJobs`, `getJob`, `listMatchable` and `splitJob`.

The survivor is the Job that has an application status, so its Tracker state, Timeline events and notes stay reachable; when neither has one it is the older Job (creation time, then id), and the kept Job keeps its discovery state. A confirm is refused with a Discovery error, and the item stays pending, only when both Jobs have an application status (one application cannot absorb another), when either Job is Dismissed or Expired (a dismissal is a marker that must stay), or when a never-merge pair (a split, or a disagreed merge that now covers the pair through a later merge) bars it. Each refusal says why and that disagree is the way out, because disagree always works and ends the item. A Job missing for the user, or two ids that already resolve to one Job (an earlier confirm folded them), is a no-op that still records the decision. The folded Job's Tracker, Timeline, notes, candidates and scores are not moved or deleted (modules never write each other's tables): they stay under its id and, since a Job with an application always survives, an application is never hidden; the folded Job has none. Every reader skips or follows the shell: `listJobs`, `getJob`, `listMatchable` (so Matching and scoring), `splitJob` and `lockJob` (so the Tracker, whose `set_status`, notes and undo take that lock, refuses a folded Job as not found) read only Jobs with no `merged_into`, and ingest follows `merged_into` to the surviving Job. A pending item that names a folded Job stays pending until decided; its Postings now show under the Job it went into.

`list_reviews` shows the Postings of both Jobs for an ai_merge item, `decide_review` applies it, and `review_stats` groups it by kind and model like the others.

A merge is an exemption from ADR 0007: it hides a Job and moves Postings without a Tracker Timeline event, and `undo_last_change` does not undo it, because it records the user's confirmation of a Review item (the Review decision is its record), not a state change of a Job. Two decisions of a chain (A with B, B with C) decided at once: the applier re-reads `merged_into` after taking the row locks and, when another decision just folded one of the two, rolls back with "decide again" and the item stays pending, so a Job is never folded into a Job that is itself folded.

The URL key chain from the #9 review is closed in two ways. Each Posting now stores its own URL key (`postings.url_key`, no backfill: no automation database holds real data), and ingest matches a new Posting's URL key against the Job's own URL key and against the URL keys of all Postings of any Job, so a Job that took a copy by company key also takes a later copy that shares that copy's URL, whatever its title (a rule merge, no AI needed). After a confirmed merge, the folded Job's keys keep working as aliases (above). A consequence: a new Posting with the URL of a Posting that a split moved out hits both the original Job and the split Job, so it is stored as a merge conflict and asked about, not silently attached.

`listMergeConflicts` is gone: the conflict rows are read inside `createAiMerge` as candidate pairs. The check that a pair is unsettled (marker, item, never-merge) is repeated under the pair lock, the item check outside its transaction, which is safe because the unique index on items is the last guard.
